Retrying a failed call is usually right. Retrying a call to a service that is down is how
a partial failure becomes a total one. Every caller politely multiplies its own traffic at
the moment the target can absorb it least.

A circuit breaker is the thing that notices and stops it. It is load shedding, applied by the
caller on behalf of the callee.

## The cost you are accepting

A breaker rejects calls that would have worked. Right after the threshold trips, part of
the dependency is still healthy, and you are failing those requests too. You accept that
because the alternative takes your own service down with the one you were calling: threads
held open on a call that will time out anyway.

## Where it does not belong

Do not put a breaker in front of a call whose failure you cannot act on. If the only
response to `CircuitOpenError` is to pass it up the stack, you have added a state machine
and changed nothing. Breakers pay off where there is a fallback: a cache, a queue, a
degraded response, a second provider.
