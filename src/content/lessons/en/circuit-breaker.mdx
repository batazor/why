      dependency gets the one thing it needs to recover, which is silence, and your own

    narration: >-

      mistake: the moment the cooldown expires, the full traffic hits a service that has
      barely stood up, it falls over again, and you have built an oscillator.
    note: >-
      One request asks the question, not the full traffic.

      If it fails the breaker goes straight back to Open and the cooldown starts over —
      note that a single failure is enough here, unlike in Closed where it takes five.

Retrying a failed call is usually right. Retrying a call to a service that is down is
how a partial failure becomes a total one — every caller politely multiplies its own
traffic at the exact moment the target has the least capacity to absorb it.

A circuit breaker is the thing that notices and stops. It is not error handling; it is
load shedding, applied by the caller, on behalf of the callee.

## The cost you are accepting

A breaker will reject calls that would have worked. Right after the threshold trips,
some fraction of the dependency is still healthy, and you are failing those requests too.
You accept that because the alternative — holding threads open on a call that will time
out — takes your own service down alongside the one you were calling.

## Where it does not belong

Do not put a breaker in front of a call whose failure you cannot do anything about. If
the only response to `CircuitOpenError` is to propagate it, you have added a state
machine and changed nothing. Breakers pay off where there is a fallback: a cache, a
queue, a degraded response, or a second provider.
